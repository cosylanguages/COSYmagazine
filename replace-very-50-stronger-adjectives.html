Stronger Adjectives: When "Very" Is Not Enough

Learn 50 stronger adjectives, how they differ in meaning and strength, and when very is still the best choice.

Read in: 🇬🇷 EL 🇫🇷 FR 🇮🇹 IT 🇷🇺 RU 🇬🇧 EN

📅 2026-09-12 ⏱️ 9 min read B1–B2 EPISODE 3

Very good and very tired are correct, natural English, and often the best choice in speech. A stronger adjective is useful when it says something more specific than "very + adjective". This guide shows 50 options, explains how they differ, and shows when to keep very.

Rule 1: some adjectives do not take "very"

Adjectives such as good, big and tired are gradable: they have degrees, so very good, quite big and a bit tired all work. Strong adjectives such as excellent, enormous, exhausted and terrifying already mean "extremely", so we do not normally say very excellent or very exhausted. With these we use absolutely, completely or really: absolutely exhausted, completely wrong.

Gradable and extreme adjectives
Gradable (use "very")Extreme (use "absolutely")Example
goodexcellentThe food was very good. / The food was absolutely excellent.
bigenormousIt is a very big house. / It is an absolutely enormous house.
tiredexhaustedI am very tired. / I am absolutely exhausted.
hungrystarvingI am very hungry. / I am absolutely starving.
coldfreezingIt is very cold. / It is absolutely freezing.

Rule 2: strong words change the meaning, not just the volume

Very sad and devastated are not the same. Devastated suggests a shock or a serious loss. Very small and microscopic are not the same either: microscopic means almost too small to see. Before you replace very + adjective, check that the new word still says what you mean, and check its register (informal, neutral or formal).

“A note on "very depressed": Depressed is a clinical term. Unless you mean a medical condition, words such as down, miserable or despondent are often more accurate in everyday conversation.”

When "very" is the right choice

  • It was a very good film, but not a masterpiece. Very good shows a high but not extreme level.
  • That's very kind of you. Very is natural and polite here. Compassionate would sound odd.
  • I'm very sorry. This is a fixed polite phrase.

Use a stronger adjective when it is more exact, not to sound more advanced.

Quiz: choose the best word

❓ The film was ___ excellent.

Excellent is an extreme adjective, so use absolutely, not very.

❓ Which sentence sounds most natural?

Exhausted already means "extremely tired". Use absolutely, completely or really.

❓ Which adjective means angry about something unfair?

Outraged describes anger at something unfair or shocking.

❓ A shop is not expensive. Which word sounds most positive?

Affordable is positive. Overpriced means too expensive for what you get.

❓ The street was ___ at midnight. Nobody was there.

Deserted means there are no people where you expect them.

❓ That's ___ kind of you, thank you.

Very is the natural choice here. The other words do not fit this fixed phrase.

Now you try

  1. Take five sentences from your journal with very. Change one only if a stronger adjective is more exact. Leave the others.
  2. Describe yesterday aloud for one minute. Use three strong adjectives and one very.
  3. Check your choices with the notes column and decide: is it stronger, more exact, or just more formal?

Summary

Keep very for everyday speech and polite phrases. Choose a stronger adjective when it is more exact, and use absolutely or completely with extreme adjectives. Always check the meaning, not just the strength.
