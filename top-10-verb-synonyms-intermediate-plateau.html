Top 10 Everyday Verbs and More Precise Alternatives

Learn when get, make, do, go, say and think are enough, and when a more precise verb says it better, with examples, grammar notes and practice.

What you will learn

By the end of this guide you will be able to choose a more precise verb when your meaning is specific, and to recognise when the simple verb is already the best choice.

Verbs such as get, make, do, have, go, say, think, look, know and give are not mistakes. They are among the most flexible words in English, and native speakers use them all day. The aim is not to replace them. It is to add precise options for the moments when get or make is too vague for what you mean.

Three rules before you start

  1. Decide the meaning first. Get can mean receive, earn, understand or arrive. Each meaning has a different, more precise verb.
  2. Check the grammar. A new verb often needs a different pattern: tell needs a person (told me), notice takes no preposition, arrive takes at or in.
  3. Ask whether the simple verb is better. If the precise verb sounds heavy or formal for the situation, keep the simple one.

The 10 verbs, organised by meaning

The tables show a common meaning of the everyday verb, a more precise option, and an example. The notes mark formal or regional uses. These are not universal synonyms: each option fits only the meaning shown.

1. Get

Get: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
get an email or a parcelreceiveI received your email yesterday. (neutral to formal; "I got your email" is also natural)
get money for workearnShe earns about £2,000 a month.
get permission or a visaobtain (formal)You must obtain a visa before you travel.
get betterimproveMy English has improved a lot this year.
get to a placereach / arrive at / arrive inWe reached the hotel at noon. / We arrived in Lyon at noon.
get someone from schoolcollect (British) / pick upI collect my daughter from school at four.
get it (understand)understandNow I understand the question.

Watch out: get + adjective (get tired, get angry, get cold) is perfectly natural: I get tired after long meetings. Become is possible but sounds more formal, or describes a lasting change: She became a doctor.

2. Make

Make: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
make dinner or a cakecook / bake / prepareI will cook dinner tonight.
make a productproduce / manufactureThe factory produces 500 cars a day.
make something newcreate / designShe designed a new logo for the café.
make a bridge or housebuildThey built a new bridge over the river.
make moneyearnHe earns a good living as a translator.
make a companyfound / set upThey set up their company in 2019.

Watch out: many make phrases are fixed and should not be replaced: make a decision, make a mistake, make progress, make an effort.

3. Do

Do: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
do a survey or experimentcarry out / conductThe team carried out a survey of 200 students.
do homework or a taskcomplete / finishI completed the exercise before class.
do a problem or complainthandle / deal withShe handled the complaint calmly.
do an examtake (British also: sit)I am taking my English exam in June.

Watch out: do the dishes, do your best, do business and do the shopping are fixed. Do is the right verb there.

4. Have

Have: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
have a meetingholdWe hold a team meeting every Monday.
have a car or a flatownShe owns two flats in Lyon.
have a problemface / experienceThe team faced several delays.
have (contain)contain / includeThe report contains three recommendations.
have luncheatWe ate lunch outside.

Watch out: I have a car is the natural way to say it. I possess a car sounds odd in conversation.

5. Go

Go: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
go to a conference or classattendShe attended the annual conference.
go to live in another countrymove / relocateHe relocated to Canada for work.
go to see a placevisitWe visited the castle on Sunday.
go away (a train or person)leave / depart (formal)The train departs at 08:30.
prices go uprise / increasePrices increased by 5% last year.
go towardsheadWe are heading to the station.

Watch out: go home, go shopping, go for a walk are natural as they are.

6. Say

Say: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
say (to a person)tell + personShe told me that she was late.
say brieflymentionHe mentioned the deadline in passing.
say why or howexplainThe teacher explained the rule to us.
say officiallyannounce / stateThe company announced a new policy.
say it is true without proofclaimHe claims that he never received the email.
say you did something wrongadmitShe admitted that she had made a mistake.
say what should be donesuggestI suggest that we start earlier.

Grammar point: you say something, but you tell someone. Say: She told me she was late. Not: She said me she was late.

7. Think

Think: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
I think (strong view)believeI believe this is the right decision.
I think (without proof)assumeI assume the train is on time.
I think (with doubt)suspectI suspect he forgot the meeting.
I think (expect)expectI expect it will rain tomorrow.
I think (reluctantly)supposeI suppose you are right.
think about (weigh up)considerWe are considering a different supplier.

Watch out: I think is natural and very common. Swapping it for in my estimation in conversation sounds stiff. Use the other verbs when they add meaning (doubt, strong belief, no proof), not to sound advanced.

8. Look

Look: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
look quicklyglanceShe glanced at her watch.
look for a long timestare / gazeHe stared at the screen. / We gazed at the sea.
look at something happeningwatchWe watched the match together.
see by chancenoticeI noticed a mistake on page two.
look carefully to checkexamine / inspectThe mechanic inspected the engine.
look + adjectiveseem / appearThe results seem promising.

Watch out: stare can feel rude; gaze is calmer, often admiring. Patterns: look at, glance at, stare at, but watch and notice take no preposition.

9. Know

These are not synonyms of know. They describe different moments or kinds of knowing, which is why they are useful.

Know: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
know someone you have seen beforerecogniseI recognised her voice immediately.
come to know (suddenly)realiseI realised I had left my keys at home.
know how something worksunderstandI understand how the system works.
know about a riskbe aware ofAre you aware of the new rules?
know a tool wellbe familiar withI am familiar with this software.
know a skill thoroughlymasterShe mastered the technique after two years.

Watch out: I know the answer needs no upgrade.

10. Give

Give: choose by meaning
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
give informationprovideThe website provides free advice.
give a job or helpofferWe offered her the job.
give a speechdeliverHe delivered the opening speech.
give money to charitydonateShe donated £50 to the charity.
give permissiongrantThe board granted permission.
give something into someone's handshandShe handed me the keys.
give a prizeawardThe jury awarded first prize to a young poet.

Grammar point: provide someone with something (provide us with training) or provide something for someone. Give me a hand is an idiom; keep it.

Three more everyday verbs

Like, use and put
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
like an activityenjoyI enjoy reading history books.
like A more than BpreferI prefer tea to coffee.
use a machineoperateHe operates the crane.
use a methodapplyResearchers applied a new method.
put something somewhereplace / leave / storeLeave the files on the desk.

Watch out: use is usually the best word, and utilise rarely adds anything. Do not turn I like reading into I am passionate about reading unless you really mean it: the meaning changes.

Formal writing: a few useful upgrades

In reports and academic writing, some everyday verbs have more precise choices. In speech with friends, the simple verb is usually better.

Formal register
Everyday verbMore precise verbExample
showdemonstrateThe study demonstrates a steady increase.
helpsupport / assistThe tool supports remote teams.
fix a machinerepairA technician repaired the printer.
fix a problemresolveThe team resolved the issue within an hour.
changeadjust / modifyWe adjusted the schedule slightly.
start a projectlaunchThe company launched a new product in May.
stop a servicediscontinueThe airline discontinued the route.
find a causeidentifyEngineers identified the cause of the delay.
askrequest / inquireShe inquired about the vacancy.

When the simple verb is the right choice

  • I got home late. Natural as it is.
  • Could you give me a hand? A fixed idiom.
  • I will make some coffee. Produce coffee would sound strange.
  • We use this app every day. Use is the best word.
  • I think it is a good idea. Natural and clear.

Rule of thumb: before you swap a verb, ask three questions.

  1. Does the new verb keep my meaning?
  2. Does it work with the same grammar (preposition, object)?
  3. Would I say or write this in this situation?

Collocations differ between languages

In English you make a decision. French says prendre une décision (literally "take"), Russian says принять решение ("accept/take"), and Greek says παίρνω απόφαση ("take"). Translating word for word causes errors, so learn verb + noun chunks, not single verbs.

Practice: choose the best verb

❓ She ___ a lot of money from her online shop.

Earn is the verb for money you get from work. Made would also be natural here, but it is not an option.

❓ You need to ___ a visa before you travel.

Obtain fits formal, official contexts. Get a visa is also fine in speech.

❓ The manager ___ us that the meeting was cancelled.

Tell needs a person after it (told us). Said us and mentioned us that are not correct.

❓ She ___ quickly at her watch and left.

Glance means a quick look. Stare means a long look, so it does not fit with quickly.

❓ I did not ___ the mistake until I read the email again.

Notice means to see something and become aware of it. The other verbs describe how you look, not the moment you become aware.

❓ We ___ this app every day to book rooms.

Use is the natural everyday word. Utilise is unnecessarily formal, and operate is for machines.

Now use it

  1. Rewrite: take five sentences from your own emails or messages that contain get, make, do, have or go. Change a verb only where it makes the meaning clearer, and leave the others alone.
  2. Speak: record a one-minute message about your last holiday or week at work. Use at least three verbs from this guide.
  3. Review tomorrow: without looking, write the more precise verb for get a visa, go to a conference, give a speech and know someone's voice. Check yourself against the tables.

Final thought: precision, not replacement

Good English is not the most complicated English. It is English where each word does its job. Keep get and make for everyday life. Reach for a precise verb when you need one.
