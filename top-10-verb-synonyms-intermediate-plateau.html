Top 10 Overused Verbs and Their Precise Synonyms for the Intermediate Plateau

Ten “safe” verbs do too much of your talking. Learn what each one really means in a sentence, which exact verb to use instead, and which traps to avoid, with maps, quizzes and a 7-day plan.

Special Edition: Breaking the B1–B2 Ceiling

Welcome to our September notebook special issue! Discover natural upgrades, stronger adjectives, and precise verb synonyms to overcome the intermediate plateau.

Say these five sentences out loud: I got an email. I got the job. I got tired. I got to the station. I got a new phone. One tiny word, five completely different meanings. A native speaker understands all five at once, but a learner who only has got sounds vague. Not wrong, just flat.

That flatness is the heart of the intermediate plateau (roughly B1–B2). Your grammar is fine and people understand you, but ten very common verbs are doing most of the work: get, make, do, have, go, say, think, look, know, give. They are not bad words, and you will keep using them every day. The trick is to notice when a more exact verb is waiting right behind them.

This article teaches one habit. Before you use a “safe” verb, ask yourself: “What exactly happened?” The answer usually hands you the better verb.

How to use this article

Every verb has its own card. Each row follows the same pattern: what you really mean → the verbs that say it → an example. The colours show where each verb belongs, so you do not use a courtroom word at a coffee shop.

The top 10 verbs

We start with the champion of vagueness: get. Use the meaning finder below, then open its card.

1

get

The champion of vagueness: one verb, at least eight meanings.

  • you receive something
    💼receive
    I got your message yesterday.→I received your message yesterday.
    Receive is a little more formal than get, perfect for emails and work.
  • you buy something
    💬buy
    I got a new phone.→I bought a new phone.
  • you earn money
    💬earn
    She gets €2,000 a month.→She earns €2,000 a month.
  • you obtain something official
    📜obtain💼secure
    He got a visa.→He obtained a visa.
    Add the idea of effort with secure: She secured a place at university.
  • something changes
    💬improve💬rise💬grow
    Things got better.→Things improved.
    Numbers go up? Use rise or grow: Prices rose last year.
  • you arrive somewhere
    💬arrive at💬reach
    We got to the hotel at six.→We arrived at the hotel at six.
    Arrive at a place, in a city. Reach needs no preposition: We reached the hotel at six.
  • you understand
    💬understand💬follow
    I don’t get it.→Sorry, I’m not following.
    I don’t get it is fine with friends. In a meeting, I’m not following sounds more polite.
  • you board a bus or train
    💬catch💬take
    I got the 8:15 train.→I caught the 8:15 train.
    Catch = you must be on time. Take = your normal way to travel: I take the bus to work.

⚠️ Trap: Get is not wrong: I got tired is natural speech. Upgrade when a more exact verb exists and when you write. I arrived to the station. → I arrived at the station.

2

make

Make means create or cause. Very often, a more exact verb is hiding inside the phrase.

  • you create something new
    💼create💼develop💼design
    They made a new app.→They created a new app.
    Develop = built step by step. Design = planned how it looks and works.
  • a company produces goods
    💼produce📜manufacture
    The factory makes bikes.→The factory produces bikes.
  • you cook or build
    💬cook💬prepare💬build
    I made dinner for everyone.→I cooked dinner for everyone.
    Build = made from parts: He built a bookshelf.
  • make + adjective = one verb
    💼simplify💼clarify💬shorten
    This app makes my life easier.→This app simplifies my life.
    She made the rules clear → She clarified the rules.
  • make + noun = hidden verb
    💬decide💬suggest💬compare💬complain
    We made a decision to move.→We decided to move.
    make a suggestion → suggest · make a comparison → compare · make a complaint → complain

⚠️ Trap: These collocations stay with make: make a mistake, make progress, make an effort, make money. I did a mistake. → I made a mistake.

3

do

Do is a blank cheque: it takes its meaning from the noun. Say the real action instead.

  • you finish a task
    💼complete💬finish
    I did the report on Friday.→I completed the report on Friday.
    Finish is the everyday word; complete sounds more official.
  • you carry out formal work
    💼carry out📜conduct
    We did a survey.→We carried out a survey.
    Carry out suits plans, tests and studies. Conduct suits surveys, interviews and experiments.
  • you deal with a problem
    💬take care of💼handle
    Don’t worry, I’ll do it.→Don’t worry, I’ll take care of it.
    She handled the complaint calmly.
  • you do sport
    💬play💬go💬do
    I do tennis on Saturdays.→I play tennis on Saturdays.
    play + ball games · go + -ing (go running, go swimming) · do + individual practice (do yoga, do judo)
  • exams, talks, meetings
    💬take💼give💼hold
    I did a presentation.→I gave a presentation.
    take an exam · give a presentation · hold a meeting (or have a meeting)

⚠️ Trap: do a photo → take a photo · do a party → have / throw a party · do a decision → make a decision

4

have

Have can mean own, contain, experience… or almost nothing at all.

  • you own something
    💬own📜possess
    My uncle has a small farm.→My uncle owns a small farm.
  • something contains parts
    💼contain💼include
    The report has three sections.→The report contains three sections.
    Include = part of a bigger list: The price includes breakfast.
  • you live through an experience
    💬go through💼experience💼face
    We had a difficult year.→We went through a difficult year.
    Face a problem · experience delays · enjoy yourself: We enjoyed the trip.
  • have + noun = hidden verb
    💬argue💬rest💬try
    We had an argument about money.→We argued about money.
    have a rest → rest · have a try → try · have a look → look
  • you eat or drink
    💬eat💬drink
    I had a sandwich.→I ate a sandwich.
    Have lunch and have a coffee are natural, so keep them. Use eat and drink when you want to be exact.

⚠️ Trap: In English you are your age and your feelings: I have 25 years. → I’m 25. · I have hunger. → I’m hungry.

5

go

Go only says “move away”. Say how you travel, why, or where to.

  • on foot
    💬walk💬stroll💬hike
    We went to the beach.→We walked to the beach.
    Stroll = slow and relaxed. Hike = a long walk in nature.
  • by transport
    💬drive💬fly💬cycle💬take
    We went to Spain last summer.→We flew to Spain last summer.
    We took the train to Madrid.
  • you attend an event
    💼attend💬visit
    I went to the conference.→I attended the conference.
    Visit people and places: I visited my grandparents.
  • you leave
    💬leave💬set off📜depart
    The bus goes at seven.→The bus leaves at seven.
    Set off = start a journey: We set off at dawn.
  • you move towards
    💼head💬make your way
    She went to the exit.→She headed for the exit.
    Make your way to = go, often through a crowd or a difficult place.
  • things develop
    💬turn out💬work out
    It went well in the end.→It turned out well in the end.
    Turn out = the final result. Work out = problems get solved: Don’t worry, it’ll work out.

⚠️ Trap: I’ll go to home. → I’ll go home. (no to before home) · I arrived to Paris. → I arrived in Paris.

6

say

Say just reports words. Pick a verb that shows why and how they were said.

  • you give information
    💼inform💬tell💬mention
    He said that the meeting was cancelled.→He informed us that the meeting was cancelled.
    Tell needs a person: He told me. Mention = say briefly: She mentioned a problem.
  • you make it clear
    💬explain💬describe
    She said how the machine works.→She explained how the machine works.
  • you make it official
    💼announce📜state
    The company said it will close two shops.→The company announced that it will close two shops.
  • you say it, but without proof
    💼claim
    He says he never saw the email.→He claims he never saw the email.
    Claim signals “maybe true, maybe not”.
  • you give reasons for a view
    💼argue💬point out💬suggest
    She said we need more time.→She argued that we need more time.
    Point out = draw attention to a fact: He pointed out a mistake. Suggest = propose an idea.
  • you say it is true / not true
    💬admit💬deny
    He said he broke the window.→He admitted breaking the window.
    Deny = say it is not true: She denied taking the money.
  • how it sounds
    💬whisper💬shout💬mutter
    She said quietly, “Be careful.”→She whispered, “Be careful.”
    Mutter = say quietly and unclearly, often when you are annoyed.

⚠️ Trap: Tell needs a person, say does not: He said me the truth. → He told me the truth. · He told that he was late. → He said (that) he was late.

7

think

Think hides how sure you are. Choose a verb that shows your level of certainty.

  • you have an opinion
    💬believe💬feel
    I think this is a good idea.→I believe this is a good idea.
    I feel that… sounds softer and more personal.
  • you are guessing
    💬suppose💬guess💬imagine
    I think he’s at home.→I suppose he’s at home.
  • you accept it without checking
    💼assume
    I thought you knew.→I assumed you knew.
  • you have a bad feeling
    💼suspect
    I think he lied.→I suspect he lied.
    Suspect usually means you believe something is wrong or dishonest.
  • you are weighing options
    💼consider💬weigh up📜reflect on
    I’m thinking about the offer.→I’m considering the offer.
  • you don’t have the answer
    💬wonder
    I’m thinking about what will happen next.→I wonder what will happen next.
  • you reach a conclusion
    💼conclude💬realise
    We thought we needed more time.→We concluded that we needed more time.
    Realise = you suddenly understand: I realised I had no keys.

⚠️ Trap: In English the not moves to think: I think it isn’t a good idea. → I don’t think it’s a good idea. · I’m agree. → I agree.

8

look

Look only says “use your eyes”. English has a different verb for every kind of looking.

  • a very quick look
    💬glance at
    She looked at her watch quickly.→She glanced at her watch.
  • you follow something that moves
    💬watch💼observe
    The scientists looked at the birds for a week.→The scientists observed the birds for a week.
    look at = something still (a photo) · watch = something that moves or happens (a film, a match) · see = it just enters your eyes.
  • a long, fixed look
    💬stare at💼gaze at
    He looked at me for ages.→He stared at me.
    Stare can feel rude. Gaze is calm and dreamy: She gazed at the sea.
  • a careful look to find out
    💼examine💼inspect💬check
    The doctor looked at my knee.→The doctor examined my knee.
    Inspect is official: An officer inspected the building. Check is the everyday word.
  • it seems to be
    💬seem💼appear
    The results look good.→The results seem promising.
    The results appear to be correct is more formal.
  • you try to find something
    💬search for💬look up
    I’m looking for my keys.→I’m searching for my keys.
    Look after a child or a pet = take care of it.

⚠️ Trap: Look needs at, and TV is for watching: Look the photo. → Look at the photo. · I look TV. → I watch TV.

9

know

Know is a state, so it hides what happened or what you can do.

  • you understand it
    💬understand💬see
    I know the problem.→I understand the problem.
  • you suddenly see the truth
    💬realise
    Now I know I was wrong.→Now I realise I was wrong.
  • you have seen it before
    💬recognise
    I know that face.→I recognise that face.
  • you have heard of it
    💼be familiar with💼be aware of
    Do you know this app?→Are you familiar with this app?
    Be aware of = you have noticed it exists: Are you aware of the risks?
  • you have a skill
    💬speak💬can💬be good at
    I know French.→I speak French.
    I can cook / I’m good at cooking, not I know to cook.
  • the moment you learn it
    💬find out💬learn💼discover
    I knew the news yesterday.→I found out the news yesterday.
    Know is a state. Use find out for the moment it happened.

⚠️ Trap: State verbs with since or for need the present perfect: I know him since 2019. → I’ve known him since 2019.

10

give

Give is correct, but a more exact verb says what kind of giving it was.

  • you provide help or a service
    💼provide💬offer💼supply
    We give free Wi-Fi.→We provide free Wi-Fi.
    Offer = say you are ready to give: She offered to help.
  • you pass something to a person
    💬hand💬pass💼deliver
    She gave me the keys.→She handed me the keys.
    Deliver = take to a place: The courier delivered the parcel.
  • an authority allows or rewards
    📜grant💼award💬allow
    They gave me permission to leave early.→They granted me permission to leave early.
    Award = a prize or a grade: She was awarded a medal.
  • you give money or things
    💼donate💬lend
    He gave a lot of money to the school.→He donated a lot of money to the school.
    Lend = you want it back: Can you lend me your pen?
  • you give advice
    💼advise💬recommend
    My teacher gave me some advice.→My teacher advised me to read more.

⚠️ Trap: Advice has no plural: He gave me an advice. → He gave me some advice. (or He advised me).

Bonus: three more “safe” verbs

Like, use and put show up in almost every learner’s speech. Here are quick upgrades for each.

+

like

“I like…” in every sentence? Show how strong the feeling is.

  • you enjoy doing it
    💬enjoy
    I like reading.→I enjoy reading.
    Enjoy is followed by -ing: I enjoy to read. → I enjoy reading.
  • you really love it
    💬love💬adore
    I like this song.→I love this song.
  • it is one of your interests
    💬be into💬be keen on💼be passionate about
    I like history.→I’m into history.
    Be keen on is common in British English. Be passionate about is strong, so it works well in interviews.
  • you choose it over another
    💬prefer
    I like tea more than coffee.→I prefer tea to coffee.
+

use

Use is useful, but you can show how and why you use something.

  • you depend on it
    💼rely on💼depend on
    We use this software every day.→We rely on this software every day.
  • you apply a rule or method
    💼apply💼follow📜adopt
    We use a simple method.→We apply a simple method.
  • you operate a machine
    💼operate💬run
    Can you use this machine?→Can you operate this machine?
  • you get the best from it
    💬make the most of💬take advantage of
    Use your time well.→Make the most of your time.
+

put

Put only says where. Say how.

  • you place with care
    💼place💬set💬lay
    Put the files on the desk.→Place the files on the desk.
    Lay = put flat: Lay the cloth on the table.
  • you keep it for later
    💼store💬keep
    I put my old photos in a box.→I stored my old photos in a box.
  • you fix it to something
    💼attach💬stick💬hang
    Put the file in the email.→Attach the file to the email.
    Hang a picture on the wall · stick a note on the door.
  • you put clothes on
    💬wear💬put on
    She put a red coat on.→She wore a red coat.
    Put on = the action. Wear = the state: She is wearing a red coat.

Put it together

Here is the same story twice. Read the “before” version aloud, then the “after”. Notice how the second one paints a clearer picture without any difficult grammar.

Everyday (B1)

BeforeYesterday I went to the city centre. I got a coffee and had a long talk with my friend. She said she got a new job. I thought it was great news. We made a plan to go and see her new office next week.
AfterYesterday I walked into the city centre. I bought a coffee and chatted with my friend for an hour. She told me she had landed a new job. It sounded like great news. We arranged to visit her new office next week.

Work (B2)

BeforeI did the report and gave it to my manager. She said the numbers looked wrong. I got the original data and we made a new version. I think it’s much better now.
AfterI completed the report and handed it to my manager. She pointed out that the numbers seemed wrong. I retrieved the original data and we produced a new version. I believe it’s much better now.

Nothing in the “after” texts is advanced grammar. The difference is vocabulary choice, and that is exactly what moves you from B1 towards B2.

Professional and academic upgrades

In emails, reports and essays, precise verbs make you sound credible. Use this table as a quick “instead of → try” reference.

Instead of ❌
Try ✅
show
demonstrate, illustrate, reveal
The graph illustrates the trend.
help
support, assist
Our tool supports remote teams.
fix
repair (things), solve (problems), resolve (issues)
We resolved the issue in an hour.
change
adjust (a little), modify (formal), transform (a lot)
We adjusted the schedule.
start
begin, launch (a product), set up (a business)
The company launched a new app.
find
locate (where), identify (what), detect (hidden)
Engineers identified the fault.
ask
request, inquire, consult
She inquired about the job.
need
require
This role requires experience.

Check yourself

Six quick questions. Tap an answer to see why it works.

❓ She ___ at her phone quickly and put it away.

Glance = a very quick look. Watch is for moving things, stare is long, and examine is careful.

❓ Which sentence is correct?

Tell takes a person directly (told me). Say needs to (said to me) and usually introduces the words themselves.

❓ Which verb shows the weakest certainty? “I ___ he’s at home, but I haven’t checked.”

Suppose means “it seems likely”. Know, be convinced and prove all express strong certainty.

❓ I ___ him since 2019.

Know is a state verb, and a state that continues until now needs the present perfect: I’ve known him since 2019.

❓ The inspector ___ the machine for safety problems.

Examine = look carefully to find out something. The others do not suggest a careful check.

❓ Which sentence is correct?

Home is an adverb here, so there is no preposition: go home, come home, get home.

Your 7-day plan

Small and speaking-first: ten minutes a day is enough.

  1. Day 1Spot. Talk or write about your day for two minutes. Underline every get, make, do, have, go.
  2. Day 2Get + make. Pick three rows on their cards and rewrite five of your own sentences.
  3. Day 3Say + think. Retell a conversation using tell, explain, suggest and suppose or believe.
  4. Day 4Go + look. Describe a short trip using walk, drive or visit, and one verb from the look scale.
  5. Day 5Do + have + give + know. Fix the collocations: take an exam, give a talk, play tennis, I’ve known…
  6. Day 6Write. Write six sentences about your week, then upgrade them with the 3-step method.
  7. Day 7Speak. Record a one-minute voice note. Use get, make and do only twice in total. Listen back and notice what felt natural.

Final thought: evolution, not elimination

Get and make are not your enemies. Native speakers use them all day. The goal is choice: knowing that a more exact verb exists, and having it ready when it helps you sound clearer, kinder or more convincing. Pick one verb this week, master its card, then move on to the next. That is how B1 becomes B2.
