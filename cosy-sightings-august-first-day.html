COSY Sightings: The Language of the First Day

Where this month’s words turned up in ordinary places — a bookshop till, a school gate, a bakery counter.

J Written by JY DM
📅 2026-08-01 ⏱️ 3 min read A1–C1

Sightings is the part of the magazine where the language stops being homework. This month we looked for the vocabulary of beginning: enrolment, timetable, term, register, first day.

A bookshop at a station in Lyon, the first week of September: a hand-written card above the till reading c’est la rentrée. Not a slogan — a fact of national life, with its own grammar. La rentrée is a noun for an event everyone attends.

A school gate in Athens at twenty to eight: a grandmother telling a child μην αγχώνεσαι, do not stress yourself. The verb is αγχώνομαι, to be strangled by anxiety. English says “don’t worry”; Greek names the thing around your throat.

A bakery in Naples the same week: si ricomincia, we start again — said by the baker about the school run, not about the bread.

Sightings are also a test of the hundred-word list. If the list is doing its job, a beginner should recognise a good share of what a city says in September: open, new, first, day, time, start. If most of a sign is still opaque, that is information too — not a failure, but a measurement of where you actually are, taken in the street rather than in a test.
