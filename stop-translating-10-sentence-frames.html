Stop Translating! 10 Reusable Sentence Frames That Let You Speak From Day One

Master 10 reusable sentence frames and 20 survival phrases that eliminate word-for-word translation, allowing beginners and intermediate learners to describe anything and think directly in English.

You're at a market. You want to ask for a charger, and the word has vanished. Your brain says: translate it! But there is a better way. Don't search for the missing word. Describe it.

This is a real skill, and native speakers use it too. With 10 short sentence frames you can describe a person, a place, an object or a moment, and the other person usually gives you the word. Then you just keep talking.

📌 The golden rule

Never stay silent. One frame plus one detail is enough. Say it, watch the face of the other person, and add a second detail only if you need to.

The 10 sentence frames

Learn the frame, not the word. Read the example aloud, then try the same frame with a new word.

#FrameExampleHelps you describe
1It's a person who…It's a person who teaches children.a person (a teacher)
2It's a place where…It's a place where people buy food.a place (a market)
3It's a thing that…It's a thing that tells the time.an object (a clock)
4It's something you use to…It's something you use to write.a tool (a pen)
5It's a time when…It's a time when people sleep.a moment (night)
6It's a day when…It's a day when people celebrate.a special day (a holiday)
7For example…I like sports. For example, football and tennis.an idea: give examples
8It means…'Huge' means very big.a word: use simpler words
9It looks like…It looks like a small computer.appearance: compare it
10How do you say… in English?How do you say this in English?any word: ask for help

See it in real life

I don't know the word, but it's something you use to write.

The waiter smiles and brings you a pen.

Context: At a café, you want to sign a bill.

Excuse me, it's a place where people buy food. Where is it?

Your listener says: “The market? It's two streets away.”

Context: In a new city, you look for the market.

It's a kind of fruit. It's yellow. It looks like a moon.

Your friend laughs: “A banana!”

Context: At a party, you forgot the word.

10 survival phrases

These are small add-ons. Put them after a frame to give more detail.

#PhraseExample
1It's a kind of…It's a kind of animal.
2It's made of…It's made of wood or metal.
3You use it for…You use it for travelling.
4It's the opposite of…It's the opposite of expensive.
5It sounds like…It sounds like music.
6It has…It has four wheels.
7It is similar to…It is similar to a car.
8It comes from…It comes from Italy.
9I don't know the word, but…I don't know the word, but it's for cooking.
10Can you repeat that, please?Sorry, could you say that again more slowly?

The 3-step method

Let's describe an umbrella without saying the word:

  1. Category: It's a thing that protects you.
  2. Function: You use it when it rains.
  3. Detail: It looks like a small roof.

Three short sentences. The listener says “An umbrella!” and the conversation continues.

Avoid these 4 traps

Stopping at “I don't know”. Add a frame instead. Silence is the only wrong answer.
Wrong verb ending. ✗ a person who teach → ✓ a person who teaches.
Too long. One frame + one detail is enough. Then ask: “Do you understand?”
Forgetting your hands. Pointing and gestures are part of real conversation. Use them!

Quick quiz

❓ You forget the word for “bakery”. Which sentence works best?

“It's a place where…” is the perfect frame for places. The listener hears bread and a shop: a bakery!

❓ Which sentence describes “scissors”?

Scissors are a tool, so use “It's something you use to…”.

❓ A friend says: “It's the opposite of expensive.” What is the word?

The opposite of expensive is cheap. Great way to explain adjectives!

Your 5-minute practice

  1. Look around you and choose 5 objects.
  2. Describe each one with one frame, without saying its name.
  3. Add one survival phrase to the two most difficult ones.
  4. Record yourself on your phone and listen to your voice.
  5. Tomorrow, repeat with 5 new objects.

Summary

Fluency is not about knowing every word. It's about never staying silent. Learn the frames, add one detail, and keep talking.

“Describe it. Don't translate it.”
