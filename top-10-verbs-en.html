Be, have, think, know, like, want, go, can, eat and drink: ten verbs, ten complete lessons. Forms, real patterns, clear diagrams, common mistakes and quizzes, built for beginners (A0–A1) with a step further for A2.

Start here: ten verbs, one toolbox

With ten verbs you can already do a lot in English. You can say who you are (be), what you have (have), what you think and know (think, know), what you like and want (like, want), where you are going (go), what you are able to do (can), and order something to eat and drink.

Each verb has its own spread: the forms, the patterns you will really use, the mistakes learners often make, and a short quiz. The ten verbs are the same ones as in the podcast episode embedded below the pages, so you can listen first, then read, or the other way round.

Verb 1 — be (am / is / are)

Be is the most useful verb in English. You use it to say who you are, how you feel, where you are, how old you are, and what things are like. In the present it has three forms: am, is, are. It needs no helper: you make a negative with not, and a question by changing the order of the subject and the verb.

Forms

SubjectFull formShort formExample
II amI'mI'm tired after work.
youyou areyou'reYou're on time.
hehe ishe'sHe's a doctor.
sheshe isshe'sShe's at home.
itit isit'sIt's cold today.
wewe arewe'reWe're ready.
theythey arethey'reThey're students.

In speech, people nearly always use the short forms: I'm, you're, he's. Use the full forms when you want to stress a word (Yes, I am!) and in short answers.

You want to say…PatternExamples

Nationality and originbe + nationality / from + placeI am French. / We are from Paris.
How someone or something isbe + adjectiveHe is tall. / I am tired. / The soup is hot.

Time, weather, priceIt is + …It is nine o'clock. / It is cold. / It is €20.
Pointing at things (A2)this / that (one) · these / those (more)This is my bag. / That is her sister. / These are our books. / Those are my parents.
Material, size, distance (A2)be + made of / numberThe table is made of wood. / The ticket is €10. / The station is two kilometres away. / My room is four metres wide.
Be or have?

This bag is mine says who the owner is. I have a car says what you own. Both are correct: they answer different questions.

Negatives and questions

Negative: put not after am / is / are. Question: put am / is / are before the subject. Never use do or does with be.

Statement (+)Negative (−)Question (?)Short answers
I am happy.I'm not happy.Am I late?Yes, you are. / No, you aren't.
You are ready.You aren't ready.Are you ready?Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
He is a doctor.He isn't a doctor.Is he a doctor?Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
It is cold.It isn't cold.Is it cold?Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
They are at home.They aren't at home.Are they at home?Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.
Two rules for short answers

1. In a positive short answer, use the full form: Yes, she is. (not Yes, she's.)

2. There is no amn't. Say I'm not. Both he isn't and he's not are correct.

There is / there are

Use there is (one thing) and there are (more than one) to say that something exists or is in a place.

One thingMore than one

Short answersYes, there is. / No, there isn't.Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.

Useful expressions with be

  • Feelings and states: I'm hungry, thirsty, cold, hot, tired, afraid. In English you are hungry — you don't have hunger.
  • Opinions and certainty: You're right. / I'm wrong. / I'm sure.
  • Abilities and interests (A2): I'm good at English. / He's interested in music.
  • Time and availability: We're late. / She's early. / I'm busy. / I'm free tomorrow.

One step further: be as a helping verb (A2)

So far, be has carried the main meaning: She is happy. Sometimes be helps another word instead. You can recognise these patterns now and learn them gradually.

PatternExampleWhat be is doing
be + -ingShe is working. / They are eating.Helps form the present continuous: an action happening around now.
be + past participleThe shop is closed. / The table is made of wood.Helps form the passive: the sentence focuses on the thing, not on who did it.

Common mistakes

Not thisSay thisWhy
I have 25 years.I am 25.English uses be for age.
She doesn't be happy.She isn't happy.No do / does with be.
Do you are ready?Are you ready?Swap the subject and be.
I am agree.I agree.Agree is a verb. No be.
He is teacher.He is a teacher.Use a / an before a job.
Yes, she's.Yes, she is.No short form in a positive short answer.

Quick quiz

❓ Choose the correct sentence.

English uses be for age: She is 25. Never use have for age.

❓ Is she at home? Choose the correct positive short answer.

In a positive short answer, use the full form: Yes, she is.

❓ ___ your parents at home?

Your parents means more than one person, so use are: Are your parents at home?

Verb 2 — have (have / has)

Have shows what you own, who is in your family, what you look like, what you eat, and some health problems. In the present simple it has two forms: have (I, you, we, they) and has (he, she, it).

Forms

SubjectPositive (+)Negative (−)Question (?)
II have a phone.I don't have a car.Do I have time?
youYou have a nice house.You don't have a pen.Do you have a question?
heHe has a sister.He doesn't have a job.Does he have a pet?
sheShe has blue eyes.She doesn't have a brother.Does she have a bike?
itIt has four legs.It doesn't have a name.Does it have a camera?
weWe have two children.We don't have time.Do we have class today?
theyThey have a new car.They don't have any pets.Do they have tickets?
QuestionShort answers
Do you have a car?Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Does he have a brother?Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.
Do they have pets?Yes, they do. / No, they don't.

What you can say with have

UseExamples
Things you ownI have a new laptop. / We have a house in Paris.
Family and friendsI have two brothers. / She has a young daughter.
AppearanceI have brown eyes. / He has a dark beard.
Features of thingsThe flat has three bedrooms. / My phone has a good camera.
Time and plansI have free time today. / She has an English lesson at ten.
Problems and illnessI have a headache. / He has a cold. / We have a problem.
Meals and activitiesI have breakfast at eight. / She has a coffee. / We have a shower. (have = eat, drink, do)

Questions with a question word use the same pattern: How many brothers do you have? What does she have in her bag? How much time do we have?

Have got (informal)

For possessions, family and appearance, many people — especially in British English — say have got. It has its own negative and question forms, without do / does.

have (with do / does)have got
+I have a car. / She has a cat.I've got a car. / She's got a cat.
−I don't have a dog. / She doesn't have a car.I haven't got a dog. / She hasn't got a car.
?Do you have a pet? / Does she have a car?Have you got a pet? / Has she got a car?
Short answersYes, I do. / No, she doesn't.Yes, I have. / No, she hasn't.
Never mix the two systems

Say Does she have a car? or Has she got a car? — not Does she have got a car?

Have got is for owning and describing. For meals and activities, use have: I have breakfast at eight. (not I've got breakfast at eight as a routine).

Everyday expressions

  • Daily life: have breakfast / lunch / dinner, have a coffee, have a shower, have a break, have a rest, have a look, have a chat.
  • Good wishes: Have a good time! Have a nice weekend!
  • Illness: have a headache, a stomachache, a toothache, a cold, a fever.
  • some / any: I have some apples. (+) / I don't have any money. (−) / Do you have any questions? (?)

Common mistakes

Not thisSay thisWhy
She have a cat.She has a cat.has with he / she / it.
She doesn't has a cat.She doesn't have a cat.After doesn't, the verb is have.
Does she has a cat?Does she have a cat?After does, the verb is have.
Does she have got a cat?Does she have a cat? / Has she got a cat?Don't mix do with got.
I have a breakfast at eight.I have breakfast at eight.No a with meals.

Quick quiz

❓ Choose the correct negative: She ___ a brother.

After doesn't, the verb returns to its base form: She doesn't have a brother.

❓ Which question is correct?

Use Has she got a car? or Does she have a car? Never mix does with got.

❓ Which sentence is correct?

For meals and routines use have, with no a: I have breakfast at eight.

Verb 3 — think

Think is how you give an opinion (I think it's good), ask for one (What do you think?) and say that your mind is busy (I'm thinking about it). It is a regular verb: think (I, you, we, they) and thinks (he, she, it).

Forms

SubjectPositive (+)Negative (−)Question (?)
II think it's good.I don't think so.Do I think too much?
youYou think I'm right.You don't think it's cheap.Do you think it's a good idea?
heHe thinks it's boring.He doesn't think it's funny.Does he think we're late?
sheShe thinks English is fun.She doesn't think so.Does she think it's expensive?
weWe think it's a great film.We don't think it's difficult.Do we think the same?
theyThey think it's too hot.They don't think it's true.Do they think it's ready?

Patterns you will use

You want to…PatternExamples
Give an opinionthink (that) + sentenceI think English is easy. / She thinks the film is long.
Say you don't agreedon't think + positive sentenceI don't think it's expensive. (This sounds more natural than “I think it isn't expensive.”)
Ask for an opinionWhat do you think? / of / about + nounWhat do you think of this song? / Do you think it's a good idea?
Answer quicklyI think so. / I don't think so."Is it open?" — "I think so." / "Is it cheap?" — "I don't think so."
Spend time thinkingthink about + noun / -ingI'm thinking about my holiday. / Let me think. / Think carefully.
Find an idea or a word (A2)think ofI can't think of the word. / Who thought of this idea?
Opinion or activity?

Opinion → present simple only: I think it's good. (not I'm thinking it's good)

Mental activity → present continuous is fine: I'm thinking about my holiday.

Common mistakes

Not thisSay thisWhy
She think it's good.She thinks it's good.-s with he / she / it.
What think you?What do you think?Questions need do / does.
I think yes. / I think no.I think so. / I don't think so.Use so as the short answer.
I'm thinking it's a good idea.I think it's a good idea.An opinion is not continuous.
I think that is not good.I don't think it's good.Put the negative on think.

Quick quiz

❓ He ___ the film is boring.

An opinion uses the present simple, and he needs -s: He thinks the film is boring.

❓ "Is it expensive?" — "I ___ so."

The natural short answer is I don't think so.

❓ Which sentence asks for an opinion?

What do you think of …? asks for someone's opinion.

Verb 4 — know

Know is for facts, information, people, places and skills. First we think, then we know! Present simple: know (I, you, we, they) and knows (he, she, it).

Forms

SubjectPositive (+)Negative (−)Question (?)
II know the answer.I don't know his name.Do I know you?
youYou know my brother.You don't know this place.Do you know the way?
heHe knows the teacher.He doesn't know my address.Does he know the time?
sheShe knows the truth.She doesn't know Peter.Does she know Anna?
weWe know this city well.We don't know what to do.Do we know the rules?
theyThey know the price.They don't know anyone here.Do they know the answer?
QuestionShort answers

Does he know the answer?Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.

What you can say with know

UsePatternExamples
Factsknow + nounI know the answer. / She knows his phone number.
Informationknow + question wordI know where he lives. / We know when the lesson starts.
People and placesknow + person / placeI know Anna. / He knows London very well.
A fact as a sentenceknow (that) + sentenceI know (that) you are tired.
Skills (A2)know how to + verbI know how to swim. / She knows how to cook.
Topicsknow about + topicI know about computers.

A question inside a question

After Do you know …? the second part has statement word order (subject + verb). There is no do / does and no swapping.

Direct questionInside "Do you know …?"
Where does he live?Do you know where he lives?
What time is it?Do you know what time it is?
Who is she?Do you know who she is?
Where is the station?Do you know where the station is?

Know or meet?

WordMeaningExample
know a personyou are familiar with themI know Peter. We work together.
meet a personyou see them for the first time, or you get togetherI met Peter yesterday. / Nice to meet you.
Know is a state, not an action

Do not use know in the -ing form: I know the answer. (not I'm knowing). To talk about slowly becoming familiar, say I'm getting to know him.

Useful phrases

  • Let me know. = Tell me. (Let me know when you arrive!)
  • get to know someone = slowly become familiar. (I want to get to know you.)
  • as far as I know = from what I know. (As far as I know, the café is open.)
  • know by heart = remember completely. (I know this song by heart.)

Common mistakes

Not thisSay thisWhy

She know my name.She knows my name.-s with he / she / it.
Do you know where is the station?Do you know where the station is?Statement word order.
I know to swim.I know how to swim.Skills need how to.
I don't know nothing.I don't know anything.One negative is enough.

Quick quiz

❓ Which sentence has the correct word order?

After Do you know …? use statement order: where the station is.

❓ She ___ how to drive a car.

With she, add -s, and know is not used in the -ing form: She knows how to drive.

❓ You have met Peter many times. Which sentence is best?

I know Peter means you are familiar with him.

Verb 5 — like

Like is for tastes: food, music, hobbies, people. It is regular — like (I, you, we, they) and likes (he, she, it) — and the -s works the same way as with have.

Forms

SubjectPositive (+)Negative (−)Question (?)
II like music.I don't like coffee.Do I like this?
youYou like pizza.You don't like tea.Do you like tea?
heHe likes sport.He doesn't like cold weather.Does he like football?
sheShe likes reading.She doesn't like cats.Does she like dancing?
weWe like travelling.We don't like waiting.Do we like this film?
theyThey like concerts.They don't like noise.Do they like cooking?
QuestionShort answers
Do you like jazz?Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Does she like dogs?Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

What can come after like?

PatternMeaningExamples
like + nounyou enjoy a thing or personI like Italian food. / She likes dogs.
like + -ingyou enjoy an activityI like swimming. / He likes cooking pasta.
like + to + verba habit or a choiceI like to read before bed. / They like to travel in summer.
I'd like + noun / to + verba polite wish right now (next spread)I'd like a coffee, please.

After like, both -ing and to + verb are usually fine. Use whichever sounds natural; after love, hate and don't mind, -ing is the safest choice.

Four questions that sound alike

QuestionIt asks about…Answer
Do you like Anna?your feeling about herYes, I do. She's great.
What does Anna like?her tastesShe likes tennis and tea.
What is Anna like? (A2)her character and appearanceShe's kind and funny.
How is Anna?her health or mood todayShe's fine, thanks.

Saying how much

  • Stronger: I really like it! / I like it a lot. / I like pizza very much.
  • A little: I quite like it.
  • Neutral: I don't mind. (It's okay for me.) / It's not my cup of tea. (I don't enjoy it.)
Word order

Keep the object next to the verb: I like pizza very much. (not I like very much pizza). And like is a state, so we say I like this song, not I'm liking this song.

Common mistakes

I'm liking this song.I like this song.Like is a state.
I like to swimming.I like swimming. / I like to swim.Choose one pattern.

Quick quiz

❓ Choose the correct sentence.

With she: doesn't + base verb like.

❓ "What is your sister like?" — She ___.

What is she like? asks for a description of her character.

❓ Which sentence is correct?

Put the object next to the verb: I like pizza very much.

Verb 6 — want / would like

Want says what you need or wish. Would like says the same thing more politely, and you will use it all the time in shops, cafés and restaurants. Want is regular: want (I, you, we, they), wants (he, she, it). Would never changes.

Want

You want to say…PatternExamples
A thing you wantwant + nounI want a glass of water. / She wants a new phone.
An action you want to dowant to + verbI want to learn English. / We want to go home.
What you want someone to do (A2)want + person + to + verbI want you to help me. / She wants him to call.
Negativedon't / doesn't wantI don't want to be late. / He doesn't want any sugar.
QuestionsDo / Does + subject + want …?What do you want? / Do you want to come? / Where does she want to go?

Would like

You want to say…PatternExamples
A polite wishI'd like + nounI'd like a table for two, please.
A polite wish for an actionI'd like + to + verbI'd like to pay, please. / We'd like to see the menu.
Offer or inviteWould you like + noun / to + verb?Would you like some tea? / Would you like to come with us?
Answer—Yes, please. / Yes, I'd love to. / No, thank you.

I'd is short for I would, not I had. With he / she / it say he'd like, she'd like — never woulds. For a negative at this level, say I don't want ….

Like, want, would like: what is the difference?

SentenceMeaning

Quick quiz

❓ You are in a restaurant. What is the most polite way to order?

I'd like …, please is polite and natural.

❓ Choose the correct sentence.

Wants takes the -s; after to, use the base verb go.

❓ ___ you like some tea?

Would you like …? is the polite way to offer something.

Verb 7 — go / be going to

Go has three jobs in this issue: go — moving from one place to another; be going — moving now; and be going to + verb — future plans. Look at these three sentences: I go to work every day. I'm going to work now. I'm going to work tomorrow. They look alike but mean different things.

Forms

FormWordExample
BasegoI go home.
he / she / itgoesShe goes home. (not gos)
Past simplewentI went home. (not goed)
Past participlegoneShe has gone home. (later grammar)
-ing formgoingI'm going home.
SubjectPositive (+)Negative (−)Question (?)
II go to school.I don't go on Sundays.Do I go there?
youYou go by bus.You don't go there often.Do you go to work every day?
heHe goes to work.He doesn't go by bus.Does he go to school?

weWe go by train.We don't go out every night.Where do we go?
theyThey go home.They don't go there.How do they go to work?

Going now: present continuous

Use am / is / are + going for movement happening now: I'm going home. She's going to the shops. They're going to Paris. Negatives and questions use be, not do: I'm not going yet. Are you going home? Where are they going?

The future: be going to + verb

Use am / is / are + going to + base verb for plans and for things you can see coming. The be changes; going to stays the same; the verb after it is always the base form.

PatternExamples
+I'm / you're / he's … going to + verbI'm going to study tonight. / She's going to buy a phone.
−I'm not / he isn't / they aren't going to + verbI'm not going to work tomorrow. / They aren't going to come.
?Am / Is / Are + subject + going to + verb?Are you going to study? / What are you going to do?
Short answers—Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. / Yes, he is. / No, they aren't.
UseExamples
Plan or intention — you have decidedWe're going to visit friends. / He's going to look for a job.
Prediction from evidence — you can see itLook at those clouds! It's going to rain. / Be careful! You're going to fall!
Listening tip

In fast, informal speech going to sounds like “gonna”: I'm gonna study. You will hear it a lot. In writing, use going to.

The tricky one: “I'm going to work”

This sentence can mean two things, because work can be a place (a noun) or a verb. Context and time words help.

SentenceMeaning
I'm going to work now.I am travelling to my workplace right now.
I'm going to work tomorrow.A plan: either I will go to my workplace, or I will do my job. Context decides.
I'm going to go to work.Clear: I plan to travel to my workplace.
I'm going to the office.Clear: movement to a place.

Quick quiz

❓ She ___ to work every day.

A routine in the present simple: she goes.

❓ Yesterday, we ___ to the cinema.

The past of go is went.

❓ Look at those clouds! It ___ rain.

A prediction from evidence: It is going to rain.

❓ ___ you going to travel tomorrow?

❓ Which sentence is correct?

Verb 8 — can

Can is a modal verb. It is different from the other nine verbs: it never changes, it is followed directly by the base verb (no to), and it makes its own negatives and questions. Use it for ability, possibility, permission and requests.

Forms

PatternExamplesShort answers

Can't and cannot mean the same. Can't is the one you will hear in conversation; write cannot as one word.

Listening tip: can or can't?

Verb 10 — drink

Drink means to take liquid into your mouth and swallow it. It works exactly like eat, with its own irregular past: drink – drank – drunk. It is also a noun: a hot drink, a cold drink, let's have a drink.

FormWordSounds like
Basedrink/drɪŋk/
he / she / itdrinks/drɪŋks/
Past simpledrank/dræŋk/
Past participledrunk/drʌŋk/
-ing formdrinking/ˈdrɪŋkɪŋ/
Careful with “drunk”

Drunk is the past participle of drink, but as an adjective it means “affected by alcohol”: He is drunk. Use drank for a finished action: I drank water.

Verbs that usually stay in the present simple

know, like, want, and have (own), think (opinion) describe states, not actions. Say I know him, I like coffee, I want some water — not I'm knowing, I'm liking, I'm wanting. But I'm having lunch and I'm thinking about it are fine, because those are actions.

What you can do now

  • ✅ Introduce yourself: name, age, job, where you are from (be).
  • ✅ Talk about things and people you have (have) and what you like and want (like, want).
  • ✅ Give an opinion and ask for one (think), and say how sure you are (know).
  • ✅ Order food and drink politely (I'd like …, Can I have …?, eat, drink).
  • ✅ Say where you go, how, and what you are going to do (go, be going to).
  • ✅ Ask for help and permission, and say what you can and can't do (can).
  • ✅ Tell a short story about yesterday, today and tomorrow.

Say it out loud

  1. Say your name, your age, your job and where you are from. (be)
  2. Say three things you have, and two things you don't have. (have)
  3. Give your opinion about your favourite song. (think)
  4. Say three things you know and one thing you don't know. (know)
  5. Say what you like, what you love and what you hate. (like)
  6. Order a drink and something to eat in a café. (want, would like)
  7. Say how you go to work or school, and where you are going to go this weekend. (go)
  8. Say three things you can do and one thing you can't. (can)
  9. Say what you ate and drank yesterday. (eat, drink)
  10. Tell a friend about your day, in five sentences. (all ten)

Final check

❓ She ___ to school every day, but yesterday she ___ at home.

A routine uses the present simple (goes); yesterday needs the past of be (was).

❓ Which sentence offers a drink politely?

Would you like …? is the polite offer.

❓ He can't ___ tonight.

Base verb after can't.

❓ We ___ going to eat at home.

Be going to needs am / is / are: We are going to eat at home.

❓ I ___ the answer, but I ___ it's difficult.

Know and think (an opinion) stay in the present simple, and I takes no -s.
